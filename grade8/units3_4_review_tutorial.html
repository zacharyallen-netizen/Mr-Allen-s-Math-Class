Part 1 · NC.8.EE.7

Multi-Step Equations

Solve in this order. 1. Distribute. 2. Combine like terms on each side. 3. Move the x-terms to one side. 4. Undo + and −. 5. Undo × and ÷. 6. Check your answer in the original.

Example: 4(x − 1) + 2x = 3x + 11
  1. 4x − 4 + 2x = 3x + 11 (distribute)
  2. 6x − 4 = 3x + 11 (combine 4x + 2x)
  3. 3x − 4 = 11 (subtract 3x)
  4. 3x = 15 (add 4)
  5. x = 5 (divide by 3)
  6. Check: 4(4) + 10 = 26 and 15 + 11 = 26 ✓

Part 2 · NC.8.EE.7

One, None, or Infinitely Many Solutions

Simplify both sides. Move the x-terms to one side.

  • One solution: the x-terms do not cancel. You get x = a number.
  • No solution: the x-terms cancel and you get a false statement, like 3 = 7.
  • Infinitely many: the x-terms cancel and you get a true statement, like 5 = 5.
Example: 2(x + 4) = 2x + 8
  1. 2x + 8 = 2x + 8 (distribute)
  2. 8 = 8 (subtract 2x)
  3. True → infinitely many solutions
5How many solutions? 6x + 3 = 6x + 3
Both sides are exactly the same. Every number works.
6How many solutions? 4x − 1 = 4x + 5
Subtract 4x: −1 = 5. That is false, so no number works.
7How many solutions? 2(x + 5) = 3x + 10
2x + 10 = 3x + 10. The x-terms are different, so they do not cancel. x = 0 is the one solution.
8How many solutions? 3(2x − 1) + 4 = 6x + 1
6x − 3 + 4 = 6x + 1. Both sides are 6x + 1, so it is always true.
Part 3 · NC.8.EE.7

Solving and Graphing Inequalities

Solve an inequality like an equation. The flip rule: when you multiply or divide by a negative, flip the sign. < and > use an open circle. ≤ and ≥ use a closed circle. Type answers like x > 4 or x <= -2.

Example: −2x + 1 ≥ 9
  1. −2x ≥ 8 (subtract 1)
  2. Divide by −2 and FLIP: x ≤ −4
  3. Closed circle at −4, shade left

Part 4 · NC.8.G.5

Parallel Lines Cut by a Transversal

Equal: corresponding, alternate interior, alternate exterior, vertical. Add to 180°: same-side interior, same-side exterior, linear pair. Interior = between the parallel lines. Alternate = opposite sides of the transversal.

Example: m∠2 = 70°. Find m∠7.
  1. ∠2 and ∠7 are alternate exterior angles
  2. Alternate exterior angles are equal
  3. m∠7 = 70°

Triangle Interior and Exterior Angles

Triangle Sum Theorem: the three interior angles add to 180°. Exterior Angle Theorem: an exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles (the two inside angles that do not touch it).

Example: A triangle has angles 40° and 65°. Find the exterior angle next to the third angle.
  1. Exterior = 40 + 65
  2. Exterior = 105°
  3. Check: third angle = 75°, and 75 + 105 = 180 ✓
