Durable Skills

The habits that help you learn in every class: sticking with hard work, working well with others, and managing yourself.

IC Imagine Middle School · 2026–27

What are durable skills?

North Carolina's Portrait of a Graduate names seven durable skills. These are skills people need in college, in a career or in the military. They "last" because they matter in every job, even as technology changes.

  • Adaptability
  • Collaboration
  • Communication
  • Critical Thinking
  • Empathy
  • Learner's Mindset
  • Personal Responsibility

IC Imagine Middle School focuses on three priority skills that come from these (the dark boxes above). Teachers teach them on purpose and score them on the same 1–5 scale as math standards. A 4 is the goal.

Self-check: under each skill, pick the level that sounds most like you right now, and a goal. Your choices stay on this device only. Talk with Mr. Allen about them.

Learner's Mindset

Perseverance

Persevere through challenges by using tools and strategies to advocate for yourself and complete academic tasks, to make sure you learn at a high level.

Social-emotional skills: self-awareness, self-management, responsible decision making

5ComplexI work through tasks and complete them on my own, even when they're difficult.
4Target (goal)I work through tasks and complete them. I may need an occasional reminder, or I ask for support.
3BasicI work through tasks with frequent teacher reminders or support.
2Partial, with supportI only work on tasks with constant teacher reminders.
1Not yet, even with supportI shut down or refuse to work on tasks.

Support can include teacher reminders, breaking the work into smaller steps, an individual work system, and targeted encouragement.

Collaboration

Peer Collaboration

Respectfully work with classmates to deepen learning and come up with ideas together.

Social-emotional skills: social awareness, relationship skills

5ComplexI actively contribute in whole-group and small-group work. I include others, encourage everyone to take part, and stay respectful, even when others see things differently than I do.
4Target (goal)I respectfully contribute and take part in whole-group and small-group work. I respond well to teacher reminders to include other voices.
3BasicI contribute in groups when the teacher reminds me to include others and to stay engaged and respectful.
2Partial, with supportI sit or work near classmates, but I contribute or interact very little.
1Not yet, even with supportI refuse, avoid or disrupt working with my classmates.

Support can include teacher reminders, breaking the work into smaller steps, grouping students by strengths, and targeted encouragement.

Personal Responsibility

Self-Regulation

Manage your emotions and behavior in class and respond well to feedback, so everyone can learn at a high level.

Social-emotional skills: self-awareness, self-management

5ComplexI use strategies to manage my emotions and adjust my behavior on my own, every time. I accept feedback and use it.
4Target (goal)I manage my emotions and behavior consistently. I need an occasional reminder and respond well to feedback.
3BasicI manage my emotions and behavior some of the time, and I respond well when I get support.
2Partial, with supportWith steady support, I accept feedback, calm down and get back in control.
1Not yet, even with supportI often act in disruptive or unsafe ways and resist support or redirection.

Support can include contact with families, conferences, restorative conversations, encouragement of expected behaviors, mentoring, and consequences such as silent lunch or detention.

How durable skills are graded

  • Each skill gets its own score from 1 to 5. It shows up on your report card.
  • A 4 is the goal. A 5 means you do it on your own, even when things are hard.
  • You will rate yourself during the year, and your teacher rates you too. Comparing the two helps you set a goal.
