Key Formulas
🔑 All volume formulas use π (pi ≈ 3.14). On the EOG you can leave answers in terms of π (like 250π) or use 3.14. These two methods give answers that differ by a little — both are correct. The decimals below use 3.14. Always include cubic units (cm³, in³, ft³, etc.).

🥫 Cylinder

V = πr²h

Base area × height

🍦 Cone

V = ⅓πr²h

One-third of a cylinder!

🏀 Sphere

V = ⁴⁄₃πr³

Only needs the radius!

⌨️ Typing answers: Each practice question tells you to use 3.14 or to leave the answer in terms of π. To type π, type π or pi (like 250π or 250pi). Type just the number — no units.
This soup can is a cylinder. Which formula finds its volume?
A can has two circle bases and straight sides, so it is a cylinder. Use V = πr²h (base area × height).
This ball has a diameter of 18 cm. What radius goes into V = ⁴⁄₃πr³? Type just the number.
r = d ÷ 2 = 18 ÷ 2 = 9 cm. So r = 9 goes into the formula.
This cone has a diameter of 8 in and a height of 9 in. Which setup is correct?
Cone → V = ⅓πr²h. Diameter 8 → r = 4. Height = 9. So V = ⅓π(4)²(9). (It works out to 48π in³.)
Try It: Change r and h

Slide the radius and the height. Watch how each shape changes, and how its volume changes. Notice: the cone is always ⅓ of the cylinder. The sphere only cares about r.

Which solid's volume formula does NOT use the height h?
Cylinder (πr²h) and cone (⅓πr²h) both use h. The sphere (⁴⁄₃πr³) uses only r.
Cylinder B is twice as tall as cylinder A. Same radius. How many times bigger is B's volume? Type just the number.
A: π(2)²(5) = 20π. B: π(2)²(10) = 40π. 40π ÷ 20π = 2. Double the height → double the volume.
Cylinder D has double the radius of cylinder C. Same height. How many times bigger is D's volume? Type just the number.
C: π(2)²(5) = 20π. D: π(4)²(5) = 80π. 80π ÷ 20π = 4. Double the radius → 4 times the volume, because r is squared (2 × 2 = 4).
Worked Examples: 🥫 Cylinders

V = πr²h. Square the radius, multiply by π, then multiply by the height.

🥫 Cylinder: r = 5 cm, h = 10 cm

V = πr²h
V = π(5)²(10)
V = π(25)(10)
V = 250π
V ≈ 785.0 cm³  (using 3.14; a calculator gives ≈ 785.4)

🔍 Spot the Error: Cylinder: r = 5, h = 10

✗ Student: V = π(5)(10) = 50π ≈ 157.1
✓ Fix: Forgot to SQUARE the radius! V = π(5²)(10) = π(25)(10) = 250π ≈ 785.4

Relationship: Cylinder, Cone & Sphere
🔑 THE BIG RELATIONSHIP: A cone is exactly ⅓ of a cylinder with the same radius and height. Two cones = ⅔ cylinder. So a cone's volume is always ⅓ of the matching cylinder.
Fill the cone with water and pour it into the cylinder. It takes 3 cones to fill it. So one cone = ⅓ of the cylinder.
A sphere that just fits inside a cylinder (the height is 2r) fills ⅔ of it.
⚠️ SPHERE SHORTCUT: If the sphere fits perfectly inside a cylinder (diameter = height = 2r), the sphere takes up exactly ⅔ of the cylinder's volume. This is why V = ⁴⁄₃πr³.
The cylinder holds 90 cm³. The cone has the SAME radius and height. What is the cone's volume?
A cone is always ⅓ of the matching cylinder: 90 ÷ 3 = 30 cm³.
The cone holds 40π in³. The cylinder has the same radius and height. Find the cylinder's volume. Leave it in terms of π.
Cylinder = 3 × cone = 3 × 40π = 120π in³.
A ball fits exactly inside a can (h = 2r). The can holds 54π cm³. Find the ball's volume. Leave it in terms of π.
Ball = ⅔ × can = ⅔ × 54π. 54π ÷ 3 = 18π, and 18π × 2 = 36π cm³. (Check: this can has r = 3, h = 6, and ⁴⁄₃π(3)³ = 36π ✓)

50π = ⅓π(5)²h → 50π = ⅓(25π)h. Multiply both sides by 3: 150π = 25πh. Divide by 25π: h = 6 ft.
Worked Examples: 🏀 Spheres

V = ⁴⁄₃πr³. CUBE the radius (r × r × r), then multiply by ⁴⁄₃ and π.

🏀 Sphere: r = 6 ft

V = ⁴⁄₃πr³
V = ⁴⁄₃ · π(6)³
V = ⁴⁄₃ · π(216)
V = ⁴⁄₃ · 216π = 288π
V ≈ 904.3 ft³  (using 3.14; a calculator gives ≈ 904.8)

🔍 Spot the Error: Sphere: diameter = 12

✗ Student: r = 12, V = ⁴⁄₃π(12³) = ⁴⁄₃π(1728) ≈ 7238.2
✓ Fix: Used DIAMETER instead of radius! r = 6, not 12. V = ⁴⁄₃π(6³) = ⁴⁄₃π(216) = 288π ≈ 904.8
💡 Always check: did the problem say diameter or radius? Diameter ÷ 2 = radius.
🔑 HEMISPHERE = half a sphere (like a bowl or a dome). Find the whole sphere, then divide by 2: V = ½ · ⁴⁄₃πr³.
Find the volume. Leave your answer in terms of π.
V = ⁴⁄₃π(3)³ = ⁴⁄₃π(27) = 36π in³. (27 ÷ 3 = 9, and 9 × 4 = 36.)
A ball has a diameter of 10 in. Find its volume. Use 3.14 and round to the nearest tenth.
r = 10 ÷ 2 = 5. V = ⁴⁄₃π(5)³ = ⁴⁄₃π(125) = 500π/3 ≈ 4 × 125 × 3.14 ÷ 3 = 523.3 in³.
This bowl is a hemisphere with radius 6 cm. Find its volume. Leave your answer in terms of π.
Whole sphere: ⁴⁄₃π(6)³ = ⁴⁄₃π(216) = 288π. Half: 288π ÷ 2 = 144π cm³.
Worked Examples: 📦 Composite & Real-World

A composite solid is made of simple solids. Find the volume of each part. Then add them.

📦 Composite: Cylinder + Hemisphere (r = 4 m, h = 10 m)

Cylinder: V = π(4)²(10) = 160π
Full sphere: V = ⁴⁄₃π(4)³ = 256π/3
Hemisphere = half: 256π/6 = 128π/3 ≈ 42.67π
Total = 160π + 128π/3 = 202.67π ≈ 636.4 m³  (using 3.14; a calculator gives ≈ 636.7)
Two equal cones are glued base to base. Find the total volume. Leave your answer in terms of π.
One cone: ⅓π(3)²(4) = ⅓π(9)(4) = 12π. Two cones: 2 × 12π = 24π cm³.
A grain silo is a cylinder with a hemisphere on top. Find its volume. Use 3.14 and round to the nearest tenth.
Cylinder: π(3)²(10) = 90π. Hemisphere: ½ · ⁴⁄₃π(3)³ = ½ · 36π = 18π. Total: 90π + 18π = 108π ≈ 3.14 × 108 = 339.1 m³.
An ice cream cone is filled, with a hemisphere of ice cream on top. Find the total volume. Leave your answer in terms of π.
Cone: ⅓π(3)²(10) = ⅓π(90) = 30π. Hemisphere: ½ · ⁴⁄₃π(3)³ = ½ · 36π = 18π. Total: 30π + 18π = 48π cm³.
Common Mistakes
❌ Forgetting ⅓ for cones — The cone formula has ⅓. Without it, you get the cylinder's volume!
❌ Using diameter instead of radius — If the problem gives diameter, divide by 2 first!
❌ Forgetting to cube for spheres — The sphere uses r³, not r². Square the radius for cylinders/cones, CUBE it for spheres.
❌ Missing units — Volume is always in CUBIC units (cm³, in³, ft³).
A student found this cone's volume: V = π(4)²(6) = 96π. What did they forget?
Cones need the ⅓. Correct: ⅓π(4)²(6) = ⅓(96π) = 32π cm³. The 96π is the matching cylinder's volume.
Mia used r = 8 for this cylinder. Find the correct volume. Leave your answer in terms of π.
r = 8 ÷ 2 = 4. V = π(4)²(5) = π(16)(5) = 80π cm³. (Mia's answer, π(8)²(5) = 320π, is 4 times too big.)
Sam wrote V = ⁴⁄₃π(9)² = 108π for this sphere. Find the correct volume. Leave your answer in terms of π.
Sam squared the radius. Spheres CUBE it: V = ⁴⁄₃π(9)³ = ⁴⁄₃π(729) = 972π ft³. (729 ÷ 3 = 243, and 243 × 4 = 972.)
