EE.1-1Approaching (3)

I can identify the base and exponent in a power and explain what an exponent represents.

Try thisIn the power 7⁴, name the base and the exponent. Then write 7⁴ as repeated multiplication and find its value.
Show answer
Answer: Base 7, exponent 4; 7⁴ = 7 × 7 × 7 × 7 = 2,401
  1. The base is the number being multiplied: 7.
  2. The exponent tells how many times the base is used as a factor: 4.
  3. 7⁴ = 7 × 7 × 7 × 7
  4. 7 × 7 = 49, 49 × 7 = 343, 343 × 7 = 2,401

EE.1-5Proficient (4)

I can simplify multi-step expressions that combine several exponent properties.

Try thisSimplify completely: (3a²b³)² · (2ab)³ ÷ (6a⁵b⁸)
Show answer
Answer: 12a²b
  1. (3a²b³)² = 3² · a⁴ · b⁶ = 9a⁴b⁶ (the 3 gets squared too).
  2. (2ab)³ = 2³ · a³ · b³ = 8a³b³
  3. Multiply: 9 × 8 = 72, a⁴ · a³ = a⁷, b⁶ · b³ = b⁹ → 72a⁷b⁹
  4. Divide by 6a⁵b⁸: 72 ÷ 6 = 12, a⁷⁻⁵ = a², b⁹⁻⁸ = b¹ → 12a²b

EE.1-8Mastery (5)

I can explain why a⁰ = 1 and why a negative exponent represents a reciprocal, connecting the rules to repeated multiplication.

Try thisUse repeated multiplication with base 5 to explain: a) why 5⁰ = 1, and b) why 5⁻² = 1/25. Then explain why a⁰ = 1 works for any base a except 0.
Show answer
Answer: 5³ ÷ 5³ = 1 = 5⁰ and 5² ÷ 5⁴ = 1/5² = 5⁻²; works for any a ≠ 0
  1. a) 5³ ÷ 5³ = (5·5·5) ÷ (5·5·5) = 1, since every factor cancels. The Quotient Rule gives 5³⁻³ = 5⁰. Both are the same expression, so 5⁰ = 1.
  2. b) 5² ÷ 5⁴ = (5·5) ÷ (5·5·5·5). Cancel 2 pairs, leaving 1/(5·5) = 1/25. The Quotient Rule gives 5²⁻⁴ = 5⁻². So 5⁻² = 1/5² = 1/25.
  3. Pattern check: 5² = 25, 5¹ = 5, 5⁰ = 1, 5⁻¹ = 1/5, 5⁻² = 1/25 — each step down divides by 5.
  4. For any base: aⁿ ÷ aⁿ = 1 and also = a⁰, so a⁰ = 1. This needs a ≠ 0 because you cannot divide by 0.

EE.1-9Mastery (5)

I can construct and evaluate complex expressions using the properties of exponents, justifying each step with mathematical reasoning.

Try thisRewrite (2⁵ · 4²)³ ÷ 8⁴ as a single power of 2, then find its value. Name the rule or reason for each step.
Show answer
Answer: 2¹⁵ = 32,768
  1. Rewrite with the same base: 4 = 2², so 4² = (2²)² = 2⁴ (Power Rule); 8 = 2³, so 8⁴ = (2³)⁴ = 2¹² (Power Rule).
  2. Inside the parentheses: 2⁵ · 2⁴ = 2⁹ (Product Rule: add 5 + 4).
  3. (2⁹)³ = 2²⁷ (Power Rule: multiply 9 × 3).
  4. 2²⁷ ÷ 2¹² = 2¹⁵ (Quotient Rule: subtract 27 − 12).
  5. 2¹⁵ = 2¹⁰ × 2⁵ = 1,024 × 32 = 32,768

Try thisWrite each number in scientific notation. a) 82,000,000 b) 0.00000415
Show answer
Answer: a) 8.2 × 10⁷ b) 4.15 × 10⁻⁶

Try thisA city has about 8 × 10⁶ people. A small town has about 2 × 10⁴ people. How many times as many people live in the city as in the town?
Show answer
Answer: 4 × 10² = 400 times as many
  1. Divide the larger by the smaller: (8 × 10⁶) ÷ (2 × 10⁴).
  2. Divide coefficients: 8 ÷ 2 = 4.
  3. Subtract exponents: 6 − 4 = 2 → 4 × 10²

EE.3-8Mastery (5)

I can choose appropriate units and use scientific notation to make sense of very large or very small real-world measurements, justifying my reasoning.

Try thisA sheet of paper is about 0.0001 m thick. The Moon is about 384,000,000 m from Earth. a) Write each in scientific notation in meters. b) Rewrite the paper thickness in millimeters and the Moon distance in kilometers (1 m = 1,000 mm; 1 km = 1,000 m). c) Which unit would you choose to report each measurement? Justify your choice.
Show answer
Answer: Paper: 1 × 10⁻⁴ m = 0.1 mm (use mm); Moon: 3.84 × 10⁸ m = 3.84 × 10⁵ km (use km)
  1. a) 0.0001 m = 1 × 10⁻⁴ m (4 hops right); 384,000,000 m = 3.84 × 10⁸ m (8 hops left).
  2. b) Paper: 1 × 10⁻⁴ × 10³ = 1 × 10⁻¹ mm = 0.1 mm. Moon: 3.84 × 10⁸ ÷ 10³ = 3.84 × 10⁵ km = 384,000 km.
  3. c) Choose a unit that is close in size to the thing being measured, so the number is easy to picture: millimeters for paper, kilometers for the Moon.
  4. Scientific notation in meters is still useful when comparing both in the same unit; the exponents show the Moon distance is about 10¹² times the paper thickness.

EE.3-9Mastery (5)

I can explain how the exponent shows the order of magnitude and use it to reason about how many times as large one quantity is than another.

Try thisAn ant has a mass of about 3 × 10⁻⁶ kg. An elephant has a mass of about 6 × 10³ kg. Kai says, "The exponents are 9 apart, so the elephant is about 10⁹ times as heavy as the ant." Explain what the exponents tell you, decide whether Kai's reasoning makes sense, and find the actual number of times as heavy.
Show answer
Answer: Kai's order of magnitude is right; the elephant is 2 × 10⁹ (2 billion) times as heavy
  1. The exponent tells the order of magnitude: each increase of 1 means 10 times as large.
  2. From 10⁻⁶ to 10³ is 3 − (−6) = 9 steps, so the masses differ by about 10⁹. Kai's estimate makes sense.
  3. For the exact multiple, also divide the coefficients: (6 × 10³) ÷ (3 × 10⁻⁶) = (6 ÷ 3) × 10³⁻⁽⁻⁶⁾ = 2 × 10⁹.
  4. The coefficients only change the answer by a factor of 2, so 10⁹ is a good estimate; the actual answer is 2 billion times.

EE.4-7Mastery (5)

I can set up and solve complex, multi-step real-world problems involving operations with scientific notation and justify each step.

Try thisEach hair on a person's head grows about 3.5 × 10⁻⁴ m per day. A person has about 1 × 10⁵ hairs. a) What is the combined length that all the hairs grow in one day? b) How many days would it take for the combined growth to reach 7 × 10³ m? Set up each step in scientific notation and justify why you multiply or divide.
Show answer
Answer: a) 3.5 × 10¹ m per day (35 m) b) 2 × 10² days (200 days)
  1. a) Every hair grows the same amount, so multiply growth per hair by the number of hairs: (3.5 × 10⁻⁴)(1 × 10⁵).
  2. 3.5 × 1 = 3.5 and −4 + 5 = 1 → 3.5 × 10¹ m per day = 35 m per day. Units: (m per hair per day) × hairs = m per day.
  3. b) To find how many days fit into the total, divide total length by length per day: (7 × 10³) ÷ (3.5 × 10¹).
  4. 7 ÷ 3.5 = 2 and 3 − 1 = 2 → 2 × 10² days. Units: m ÷ (m per day) = days.
  5. Check: 35 m per day × 200 days = 7,000 m = 7 × 10³ m ✓

EE.4-8Mastery (5)

I can interpret the reasonableness of a result and choose appropriate units for the answer.

Try thisAn Olympic pool holds about 2.5 × 10³ m³ of water. Each cubic meter of water has a mass of 1 × 10³ kg. Riley says the water's mass is 2.5 × 10⁹ kg. a) Is Riley's answer reasonable? Explain. b) Find the correct mass in kilograms. c) Rewrite the mass in metric tons (1 metric ton = 1 × 10³ kg) and explain why that unit is a good choice.
Show answer
Answer: Not reasonable; correct mass is 2.5 × 10⁶ kg = 2.5 × 10³ metric tons (2,500 t)
  1. a) Estimate: about 2,500 m³ × 1,000 kg each = about 2,500,000 kg. Riley's 2.5 × 10⁹ kg (2.5 billion kg) is 1,000 times too big, so it is not reasonable.
  2. Riley multiplied the exponents (3 × 3 = 9). When multiplying powers of 10 you ADD them.
  3. b) (2.5 × 10³)(1 × 10³) = 2.5 × 10³⁺³ = 2.5 × 10⁶ kg
  4. c) (2.5 × 10⁶) ÷ (1 × 10³) = 2.5 × 10³ metric tons = 2,500 t.
  5. Metric tons are a better unit because the number (2,500) is easier to picture than 2,500,000 kg.
