G.5-1Approaching (3)

I can use complementary, supplementary, vertical, and adjacent angle relationships to find missing angles.

Try thisLines AB and CD cross at point E. ∠AEC = 58°. a) Find ∠BED, ∠AED, and ∠CEB. b) Separately, ∠P and ∠Q are complementary and ∠P = 34°. Find ∠Q.
Show answer
Answer: ∠BED = 58°, ∠AED = 122°, ∠CEB = 122°, ∠Q = 56°
  1. ∠BED is vertical to ∠AEC, so ∠BED = 58°.
  2. ∠AED and ∠AEC are adjacent and form a linear pair: 180° − 58° = 122°.
  3. ∠CEB is vertical to ∠AED, so ∠CEB = 122°.
  4. Complementary angles add to 90°: ∠Q = 90° − 34° = 56°.

G.5-2Approaching (3)

I can identify the angle pairs formed when parallel lines are cut by a transversal (corresponding, alternate interior, alternate exterior, same-side interior) and state whether they are congruent or supplementary.

Try thisLines j and k are parallel (j ∥ k) and are cut by transversal t. Line j is above line k. The table shows where the eight angles are. For each pair, name the angle pair type and say whether the angles are congruent or supplementary. a) ∠2 and ∠6 b) ∠4 and ∠5 c) ∠1 and ∠8 d) ∠4 and ∠6
LineAbove-left of tAbove-right of tBelow-left of tBelow-right of t
j (top line)∠1∠2∠3∠4
k (bottom line)∠5∠6∠7∠8
Show answer
Answer: a) corresponding, congruent; b) alternate interior, congruent; c) alternate exterior, congruent; d) same-side interior, supplementary
  1. a) Same position at each crossing (above-right) → corresponding → congruent.
  2. b) Both between the lines, on opposite sides of t → alternate interior → congruent.
  3. c) Both outside the lines, on opposite sides of t → alternate exterior → congruent.
  4. d) Both between the lines, on the same side of t → same-side interior → supplementary (add to 180°).

G.5-3Approaching (3)

I can apply the Triangle Sum Theorem (interior angles sum to 180°) to find a missing angle.

Try thisA triangular sail is shaped like △RST. ∠R = 47° and ∠S = 76°. What is the measure of ∠T?
Show answer
Answer: ∠T = 57°
  1. Triangle Angle Sum: ∠R + ∠S + ∠T = 180°
  2. 47 + 76 + ∠T = 180 → 123 + ∠T = 180
  3. ∠T = 180 − 123 = 57°

G.5-4Approaching (3)

I can apply the Exterior Angle Theorem (an exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles).

Try thisIn △KLM, side KM is extended past M to point N. This makes exterior angle ∠LMN. ∠K = 38° and ∠L = 81°. Find ∠LMN.
Show answer
Answer: ∠LMN = 119°
  1. The remote (far) interior angles for the exterior angle at M are ∠K and ∠L.
  2. Exterior Angle Theorem: ∠LMN = ∠K + ∠L = 38° + 81° = 119°
  3. Check: ∠KML = 180 − 38 − 81 = 61°, and 61° + 119° = 180° (linear pair) ✓

G.5-5Approaching (3)

I can use the Angle-Angle (AA) criterion to determine whether two triangles are similar.

Try this△ABC has ∠A = 32° and ∠B = 104°. △XYZ has ∠X = 104° and ∠Z = 44°. Are the triangles similar? Use the AA criterion to explain.
Show answer
Answer: Yes, similar by AA: △ABC ~ △YXZ
  1. Find ∠C: 180 − 32 − 104 = 44°.
  2. Two pairs of angles match: ∠B = ∠X = 104° and ∠C = ∠Z = 44°.
  3. By AA the triangles are similar (and ∠A = ∠Y = 32°), so △ABC ~ △YXZ.

G.5-6Proficient (4)

I can write and solve an equation using an angle relationship to find an unknown value, including using more than one angle relationship together to solve a multi-step problem.

Try thisLines m and n are parallel and are cut by transversal t. ∠1 is above line m on the left side of t. ∠5 is above line n on the left side of t, so ∠1 and ∠5 are corresponding angles. ∠6 is above line n on the right side of t, so ∠5 and ∠6 form a linear pair. ∠1 = (4x + 12)° and ∠6 = (2x + 30)°. Find x, ∠1, and ∠6.
Show answer
Answer: x = 23; ∠1 = 104°, ∠6 = 76°
  1. Corresponding angles are equal, so ∠5 = ∠1 = (4x + 12)°.
  2. ∠5 and ∠6 are a linear pair: (4x + 12) + (2x + 30) = 180
  3. Combine: 6x + 42 = 180 → 6x = 138 → x = 23
  4. ∠1 = 4(23) + 12 = 104°, ∠6 = 2(23) + 30 = 76°
  5. Check: 104° + 76° = 180° ✓

G.5-7Proficient (4)

I can analyze and correct errors in another student's use of an angle relationship.

Try thisLines a and b are parallel and are cut by transversal c. Two same-side interior angles measure (5x − 20)° and (3x + 40)°. Marcus wrote 5x − 20 = 3x + 40, got x = 30, and said each angle is 130°. Find his error and give the correct x and angle measures.
Show answer
Answer: Error: same-side interior angles are supplementary, not equal. x = 20; angles are 80° and 100°.
  1. Marcus set the angles equal, but same-side interior angles add to 180°.
  2. His answer fails: 130° + 130° = 260°, not 180°.
  3. Correct equation: (5x − 20) + (3x + 40) = 180 → 8x + 20 = 180 → 8x = 160 → x = 20
  4. Angles: 5(20) − 20 = 80° and 3(20) + 40 = 100°. Check: 80° + 100° = 180° ✓

G.5-8Mastery (5)

I can use an informal argument to justify why an angle relationship must be true (for example, why alternate interior angles are congruent, or why the interior angles of a triangle sum to 180°).

Try thisStart with any triangle △ABC. Through vertex B, draw line ℓ parallel to side AC. At point B, line ℓ and the triangle make three angles side by side: ∠1 (between ℓ and side BA), ∠2 (which is ∠ABC), and ∠3 (between side BC and ℓ). Use these angles and parallel-line relationships to explain why ∠A + ∠ABC + ∠C = 180° for every triangle.
Show answer
Answer: ∠1 + ∠2 + ∠3 = 180° on line ℓ, and ∠1 = ∠A, ∠3 = ∠C, so ∠A + ∠ABC + ∠C = 180°.
  1. ∠1, ∠2, and ∠3 sit side by side along straight line ℓ, so ∠1 + ∠2 + ∠3 = 180°.
  2. Side AB is a transversal of ℓ ∥ AC, so ∠1 and ∠A are alternate interior angles → ∠1 = ∠A.
  3. Side BC is also a transversal, so ∠3 and ∠C are alternate interior angles → ∠3 = ∠C.
  4. Substitute: ∠A + ∠ABC + ∠C = 180°.
  5. Nothing depended on the triangle's shape, so this is true for every triangle.

G.5-9Mastery (5)

I can solve complex problems that combine several angle relationships and justify each step.

Try thisLines p and q are parallel, with p above q. Point A is on line p, with point D on p to the left of A and point E on p to the right of A. Points B and C are on line q, with B to the left of C, so △ABC sits between the lines. Point F is on line q to the right of C. ∠DAB = (2x + 10)°, ∠BAC = 55°, and ∠EAC = (3x − 5)°. Find x, ∠ABC, ∠ACB, and the exterior angle ∠ACF. Justify each step with an angle relationship.
Show answer
Answer: x = 24; ∠ABC = 58°, ∠ACB = 67°, ∠ACF = 113°
  1. ∠DAB, ∠BAC, ∠EAC form a straight angle on line p: (2x + 10) + 55 + (3x − 5) = 180 → 5x + 60 = 180 → x = 24. So ∠DAB = 58° and ∠EAC = 67°.
  2. ∠ABC and ∠DAB are alternate interior angles (p ∥ q, transversal AB) → ∠ABC = 58°.
  3. ∠ACB and ∠EAC are alternate interior angles (transversal AC) → ∠ACB = 67°.
  4. Exterior Angle Theorem: ∠ACF = ∠ABC + ∠BAC = 58° + 55° = 113°.
  5. Check: 58° + 55° + 67° = 180° (Triangle Sum) and 67° + 113° = 180° (linear pair) ✓
