G.9-1Approaching (3)

I can identify the radius, diameter, and height of cylinders, cones, and spheres.

Try thisFill in the radius (r), diameter (d), and height (h) for each object. Write "none" if the solid has no height.
ObjectShapeGiven
Soup canCylinderdiameter 8 cm, height 11 cm

Answer: Can: r = 4 cm, d = 8 cm, h = 11 cm; Cone: r = 15 cm, d = 30 cm, h = 45 cm; Ball: r = 12 cm, d = 24 cm, no height
  1. Radius = diameter ÷ 2, and diameter = radius × 2.
  2. Can: r = 8 ÷ 2 = 4 cm. Cone: d = 15 × 2 = 30 cm.
  3. Ball: r = 24 ÷ 2 = 12 cm. A sphere has no height in its formula (V = ⁴⁄₃πr³).

Try thisA candle is shaped like a cylinder with a radius of 3 cm and a height of 8 cm. Find its volume. Give your answer in terms of π and rounded to the nearest tenth (use π ≈ 3.14).
Show answer
Answer: 72π cm³ ≈ 226.1 cm³
  1. V = πr²h
  2. V = π(3)²(8) = π(9)(8) = 72π
  3. 72 × 3.14 = 226.08 ≈ 226.1 cm³

G.9-5Approaching (3)

I can give an answer in terms of π or rounded, labeled in cubic units.

Try thisA water tower tank is a cylinder with a radius of 3 m and a height of 10 m. a) Give its volume in terms of π. b) Give its volume rounded to the nearest tenth using π ≈ 3.14. c) Jordan wrote "282.6 m²." What is wrong with Jordan's label?
Show answer
Answer: a) 90π m³; b) ≈ 282.6 m³; c) volume needs cubic units (m³), not m²
  1. V = πr²h = π(3)²(10) = π(9)(10) = 90π m³.
  2. 90 × 3.14 = 282.6 m³.
  3. m² measures area. Volume fills space in 3 directions, so it is in cubic units: m³.

G.9-6Proficient (4)

I can explain that a cone holds ⅓ the volume of a cylinder with the same base and height, and a sphere holds ⅔ the volume of the cylinder that fits around it.

Try thisA tube-shaped gift box is a cylinder with a radius of 6 cm and a height of 12 cm. Give each volume in terms of π and rounded to the nearest tenth (use π ≈ 3.14). a) Find the volume of the cylinder. b) A cone has the same radius and height. Find its volume and explain how it compares to the cylinder. c) A ball with a radius of 6 cm fits exactly inside the box. Find its volume and explain how it compares to the cylinder.
Show answer
Answer: Cylinder 432π ≈ 1356.5 cm³; cone 144π ≈ 452.2 cm³ (⅓); ball 288π ≈ 904.3 cm³ (⅔)
  1. Cylinder: π(6)²(12) = π(36)(12) = 432π ≈ 1356.5 cm³.
  2. Cone: ⅓π(6)²(12) = 144π ≈ 452.2 cm³. It is ⅓ of the cylinder: 3 cones fill the cylinder.
  3. Ball: ⁴⁄₃π(6)³ = ⁴⁄₃π(216) = 288π ≈ 904.3 cm³.
  4. The ball fits exactly (h = 2r = 12), and 288π ÷ 432π = ⅔, so the sphere holds ⅔ of the cylinder.

G.9-7Proficient (4)

I can solve real-world volume problems, including ones where the diameter is given, and analyze and correct errors such as using the diameter as the radius or leaving out the ⅓.

Try thisA planter is shaped like a cone with a diameter of 12 in and a height of 10 in. Riley found its volume this way: V = π(12)²(10) = 1440π in³. Find Riley's two mistakes and give the correct volume. Give the correct volume in terms of π and rounded to the nearest tenth (use π ≈ 3.14).
Show answer
Answer: Correct volume: 120π in³ ≈ 376.8 in³
  1. Mistake 1: Riley used the diameter as the radius. r = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 in.
  2. Mistake 2: Riley left out the ⅓ for a cone.
  3. V = ⅓π(6)²(10) = ⅓π(36)(10) = ⅓(360π) = 120π
  4. 120 × 3.14 = 376.8 in³. (Riley's answer is 12 times too big.)

I can find a missing radius or height when I know the volume.

Try thisa) An oatmeal canister is a cylinder that holds 360π cm³. Its radius is 6 cm. Find its height. b) A pile of sand is shaped like a cone. It holds 48π ft³ and is 9 ft tall. Find its radius.
Show answer
Answer: a) h = 10 cm; b) r = 4 ft
  1. a) 360π = π(6)²h → 360π = 36πh.
  2. Divide both sides by 36π: h = 10 cm.
  3. b) 48π = ⅓πr²(9) → 48π = 3πr².
  4. Divide by 3π: r² = 16, so r = √16 = 4 ft.

G.9-9Mastery (5)

I can solve multi-step problems involving composite solids made of cylinders, cones, and spheres or hemispheres, justifying each step.

Try thisA toy rocket is made of a cylinder body with a cone on top for the nose. The body and the nose both have a diameter of 6 cm. The body is 20 cm tall, and the nose cone is 4 cm tall. Find the total volume of the rocket. Justify each step. Give your answer in terms of π and rounded to the nearest tenth (use π ≈ 3.14).
Show answer
Answer: 192π cm³ ≈ 602.9 cm³
  1. The rocket is a composite solid, so find each part's volume and add. Diameter 6 → r = 3 cm for both parts.
  2. Cylinder body: V = πr²h = π(3)²(20) = 180π.
  3. Cone nose: V = ⅓πr²h = ⅓π(3)²(4) = 12π.
  4. Total: 180π + 12π = 192π.
  5. 192 × 3.14 = 602.88 ≈ 602.9 cm³.

G.9-10Mastery (5)

I can explain how the volume changes when the radius or height is scaled, and why changing the radius has a greater effect.

Try thisA cone has a radius of 3 in and a height of 8 in. Give each volume in terms of π and rounded to the nearest tenth (use π ≈ 3.14). a) Find its volume. b) Find the volume if the height is tripled (radius stays 3 in). c) Find the volume if the radius is tripled (height stays 8 in). d) Explain why tripling the radius changes the volume more than tripling the height.
Show answer
Answer: a) 24π ≈ 75.4 in³; b) 72π ≈ 226.1 in³ (×3); c) 216π ≈ 678.2 in³ (×9)
  1. a) ⅓π(3)²(8) = ⅓π(9)(8) = 24π ≈ 75.4 in³.
  2. b) ⅓π(3)²(24) = 72π ≈ 226.1 in³, which is 3 times as much.
  3. c) ⅓π(9)²(8) = ⅓π(81)(8) = 216π ≈ 678.2 in³, which is 9 times as much.
  4. Height is used once in the formula, so ×3 height → ×3 volume.
  5. Radius is squared (r · r), so ×3 radius → ×3 × 3 = ×9 volume. In general, multiplying r by k multiplies V by k².
