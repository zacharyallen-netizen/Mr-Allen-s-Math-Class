G.6-1Approaching (3)

I can state the Pythagorean Theorem (a² + b² = c²) and identify the legs and hypotenuse of a right triangle.

Try thisA right triangle has sides of 12 cm, 35 cm, and 37 cm. The right angle is between the 12 cm side and the 35 cm side. Name the legs and the hypotenuse, then write the Pythagorean Theorem using these side lengths.
Show answer
Answer: Legs: 12 cm and 35 cm; hypotenuse: 37 cm; 12² + 35² = 37²
  1. The legs are the two sides that form the right angle: 12 cm and 35 cm.
  2. The hypotenuse is across from the right angle and is the longest side: 37 cm.
  3. a² + b² = c² → 12² + 35² = 37².
  4. Check: 144 + 1225 = 1369 and 37² = 1369 ✓

G.6-5Proficient (4)

I can use a diagram or area model to explain why the Pythagorean Theorem is true.

Try thisA large square has sides of 23 units. Inside it, four copies of a right triangle with legs 8 and 15 are placed in the corners. They leave a tilted square in the middle whose side is the hypotenuse, c. Use areas to find c² and show that this matches 8² + 15².
Show answer
Answer: c² = 529 − 240 = 289, which equals 8² + 15² = 289, so c = 17
  1. Area of large square: 23² = 529 (each side is 8 + 15 = 23).
  2. Area of one triangle: ½ × 8 × 15 = 60. Four triangles: 4 × 60 = 240.
  3. Tilted square = large square − triangles: c² = 529 − 240 = 289.
  4. Legs: 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289. Same area, so a² + b² = c² (c = √289 = 17).

G.6-8Mastery (5)

I can give a proof or informal argument for the Pythagorean Theorem and explain how the converse follows from it.

Try thisFour copies of a right triangle with legs a and b and hypotenuse c fit in a square with sides a + b in two ways. Way 1: the triangles sit in the corners and leave one tilted square of side c. Way 2: the triangles are paired into two rectangles, leaving one square of side a and one square of side b. Use these to explain why a² + b² = c². Then explain why the converse is true.
Show answer
Answer: Same big square minus the same four triangles leaves c² in Way 1 and a² + b² in Way 2, so a² + b² = c². Converse: a right triangle with legs a and b has hypotenuse c, so it has the same three sides as the given triangle and matches it exactly; the given triangle has a right angle too.
  1. Both big squares have the same area, (a + b)², and both have the same four triangles taken out.
  2. So the leftover areas must be equal: the c² square in Way 1 equals the a² and b² squares in Way 2. That gives a² + b² = c².
  3. Converse: suppose a triangle has sides a, b, c with a² + b² = c². Build a separate right triangle with legs a and b.
  4. By the theorem, that right triangle's hypotenuse squared is a² + b² = c², so its hypotenuse is c.
  5. The two triangles have the same three side lengths, so they match exactly (one fits right on top of the other). So the first triangle also has a right angle, across from side c.

G.6-9Mastery (5)

I can explain the difference between the theorem and its converse and describe when each is used.

Try thisExplain the difference between the Pythagorean Theorem and its converse. Then tell which one you would use in each case, and why. (A) A wheelchair ramp rises 1 ft (straight up) over a horizontal distance of 12 ft along the ground. How long is the ramp, to the nearest tenth of a foot? (B) A builder measures a wall corner as 6 ft, 8 ft, and 10 ft and wants to know if it is square.
Show answer
Answer: Theorem: right triangle → a² + b² = c² (use it to find a side, case A). Converse: a² + b² = c² → right triangle (use it to test, case B).
  1. Theorem: IF a triangle is right, THEN a² + b² = c². You already know it is right and use it to find a missing side.
  2. Converse: IF a² + b² = c², THEN the triangle is right. You know all three sides and use it to test for a right angle.
  3. (A) uses the theorem: the vertical rise meets the ground at a right angle, and the ramp length is unknown (1² + 12² = c², c = √145 ≈ 12.0 ft).
  4. (B) uses the converse: all sides are known and the right angle is in question (6² + 8² = 100 = 10², so it is square).

G.7-2Approaching (3)

I can explain that the Pythagorean Theorem applies only to right triangles.

Try thisA triangle has two sides of 5 cm and 6 cm, and the angle between them is 80°. Kayla uses a² + b² = c² and says the third side is √61 cm. Is her method correct? Explain.
Show answer
Answer: No; the angle between the 5 cm and 6 cm sides is 80°, not 90°, so it is not a right triangle and the Pythagorean Theorem does not apply.
  1. The Pythagorean Theorem only works on RIGHT triangles (one 90° angle), with a and b as the legs that form the right angle.
  2. Here the angle between the 5 cm and 6 cm sides is 80°, not 90°.
  3. So 5 and 6 are not legs of a right triangle, and a² + b² = c² cannot be used to find the third side. (Because 80° is less than 90°, the third side is actually shorter than √61 cm.)

G.7-7Mastery (5)

I can apply the Pythagorean Theorem to solve complex, multi-step problems in various contexts, including 2D & 3D figures, with clear mathematical justification.

Try thisA shipping box is 12 in long, 9 in wide, and 8 in tall. Will an 18-inch umbrella fit inside lying straight, from a bottom corner to the opposite top corner? Show each step and explain.
Show answer
Answer: No; the longest diagonal is 17 in, which is less than 18 in.
  1. Step 1: The diagonal across the bottom is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs 12 and 9: 12² + 9² = 144 + 81 = 225, so it is √225 = 15 in.
  2. Step 2: That bottom diagonal and the 8 in height form a new right triangle, with the right angle where the height meets the floor.
  3. Step 3: 15² + 8² = 225 + 64 = 289, so the longest diagonal is √289 = 17 in.
  4. 17 in < 18 in, so the umbrella will not fit.

G.8-6Mastery (5)

Try thisPoint A is at (1, 2, 3) and point B is at (7, 5, 5) in 3D space (x, y, z). Find the distance AB. Explain your method and write a general rule for the distance between two points in 3D.
Show answer
Answer: AB = 7 units; rule: d² = Δx² + Δy² + Δz²
  1. Changes: Δx = 7 − 1 = 6, Δy = 5 − 2 = 3, Δz = 5 − 3 = 2.
  2. Flat (x-y) right triangle: legs 6 and 3, so the flat diagonal² = 36 + 9 = 45.
  3. That flat diagonal and the height Δz = 2 form a second right triangle: d² = 45 + 2² = 49.
  4. d = √49 = 7. Using the theorem twice gives the rule d² = Δx² + Δy² + Δz², so d = √(Δx² + Δy² + Δz²).
