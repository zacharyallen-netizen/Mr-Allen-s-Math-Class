1 The Pythagorean Theorem

🔑 THE THEOREM: In a RIGHT triangle, the sum of the squares of the two LEGS equals the square of the HYPOTENUSE: a² + b² = c².

a, b = legs (shorter sides) · c = hypotenuse (longest side, across from the right angle)

The two smaller squares (a²+b²) EXACTLY fill the biggest square (c²)
⚠️ IMPORTANT: The Pythagorean Theorem ONLY works on RIGHT triangles (triangles with a 90° angle)!

Look at the right triangle. Which side is the hypotenuse?
The hypotenuse is across from the right angle (the little square). It is always the longest side. Here it is 10.
A triangle has angles of 50°, 60°, and 70°. Can you use a² + b² = c² on it?
No angle is 90°, so it is not a right triangle. The theorem only works on RIGHT triangles. Answer: No.
The squares on the two legs of a right triangle have areas 36 and 64. What is the area of the square on the hypotenuse?
a² + b² = c² → 36 + 64 = c² → c² = 100. (So the hypotenuse is √100 = 10.)
The square on the hypotenuse has area 169. The square on one leg has area 25. What is the area of the square on the other leg?
a² + 25 = 169 → a² = 169 − 25 = 144. (So that leg is √144 = 12.)

3 Finding a Missing Leg

🔑 WHEN TO USE: When you know ONE LEG and the HYPOTENUSE. Formula: a² = c² − b² → a = √(c² − b²)
⚠️ REMEMBER: Finding a LEG = SUBTRACT from the hypotenuse squared. Finding the HYPOTENUSE = ADD the two legs squared.

Example: One leg is 5, hypotenuse is 13. Find the other leg.

Step 1 — Write formula: click to reveal a² + b² = c²
Step 2 — Substitute: click to reveal a² + 5² = 13²
Step 3 — Square: click to reveal a² + 25 = 169
Step 4 — Subtract 25 (finding a LEG = SUBTRACT): click to reveal a² = 144
✓ Step 5 — Square root: click to reveal a = √144 = 12
ERROR ANALYSIS A student finds a missing leg when c = 13, b = 5 by writing a² = 13² + 5² = 194. What's wrong?
To find a leg you SUBTRACT: a² = c² − b² = 169 − 25 = 144 → a = 12. Adding is only for the hypotenuse.
One leg is 6 and the hypotenuse is 10. Find the other leg.
6² + b² = 10² → 36 + b² = 100 → b² = 100 − 36 = 64 → b = √64 = 8
One leg is 7 and the hypotenuse is 12. Find the other leg. Round to the nearest tenth (or give the exact √ answer).
7² + b² = 12² → 49 + b² = 144 → b² = 95 → b = √95 ≈ 9.747 ≈ 9.7
A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 26 and one leg of 10. What is its perimeter?
Missing leg: 10² + b² = 26² → 100 + b² = 676 → b² = 576 → b = 24. Perimeter = 10 + 24 + 26 = 60

4 Converse of the Pythagorean Theorem

🔑 THE CONVERSE: Given three side lengths a ≤ b ≤ c, test: does a² + b² = c²? If YES → right triangle. If NO → not a right triangle. Always test the two SMALLER sides against the LARGEST.

Example: Sides 9, 12, 15. Is it a right triangle?

Step 1 — Order the sides: click to reveal 9 ≤ 12 ≤ 15, so the LARGEST is c = 15
Step 2 — Write the test: click to reveal does a² + b² = c²?
Step 3 — Substitute the two SMALLER sides: click to reveal 9² + 12² =? 15²
Step 4 — Square each one: click to reveal 81 + 144 =? 225
Step 5 — Add the left side: click to reveal 225 =? 225
✓ Step 6 — Compare and conclude: click to reveal The two sides are EQUAL, so YES — it is a right triangle.
⚠️ Your eyes cannot do this job. Every triangle below is drawn to true scale. Look at 11, 13, 17: it is not a right triangle, because 11² + 13² = 121 + 169 = 290 while 17² = 289. It misses by one. Drawn accurately it sits two-tenths of a degree off square — nobody alive could spot that. This is exactly why the converse exists: you must run the arithmetic, every time.

6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100 and 10² = 100. They are equal, so Yes.
A triangle has sides 17, 8, and 15. Is it a right triangle?
Put the sides in order: 8, 15, 17. Test: 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289 and 17² = 289. Equal, so Yes.
A triangle has sides 7, 9, and 12. Is it a right triangle?
7² + 9² = 49 + 81 = 130, but 12² = 144. 130 ≠ 144, so No.
A triangle has sides 9, 40, and x. The side x is the longest. What must x be for a right triangle?
For a right triangle, 9² + 40² = x² → 81 + 1600 = 1681 → x = √1681 = 41

Ladder Against a Wall: A ladder 15 ft long leans against a wall. The base is 9 ft from the wall. How high does it reach?

Setup: click to reveal 9² + h² = 15²
Square & subtract: click to reveal 81 + h² = 225 → h² = 144
✓ Square root: click to reveal h = √144 = 12 ft
A rectangular garden is 15 m long and 8 m wide. A path goes from one corner to the opposite corner. How long is the path?
The path is the hypotenuse. 8² + 15² = c² → 64 + 225 = 289 → c = √289 = 17 m
A 10-foot ladder leans against a wall. The bottom is 6 feet from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?
The ladder is the hypotenuse. 6² + h² = 10² → 36 + h² = 100 → h² = 64 → h = 8 ft
Sam walks 12 blocks east, then 9 blocks north. How many blocks shorter is the straight path back to where he started?
Straight path: 12² + 9² = 144 + 81 = 225 → √225 = 15 blocks. He walked 12 + 9 = 21 blocks. 21 − 15 = 6 blocks shorter.

6 Distance on the Coordinate Plane

📐 HOW IT WORKS: Connect the two points to form a right triangle. Δx = |x₂ − x₁| and Δy = |y₂ − y₁|. Then use a² + b² = c² where a = Δx, b = Δy, and c = distance.
⚠️ NO DISTANCE FORMULA NEEDED! You already know how to do this — just use the Pythagorean Theorem!

Example: Distance from (0, −2) to (6, 2)

Δx: click to reveal |6 − 0| = 6
Δy: click to reveal |2 − (−2)| = 4
Setup: click to reveal Δx² + Δy² = d² → 36 + 16 = 52
✓ Answer: click to reveal d = √52 ≈ 7.21
⚠️ WATCH YOUR SIGNS: When subtracting negative coordinates, subtracting a negative means ADDING! Example: 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8, NOT 5 − 3 = 2.
Find the distance between (1, 1) and (9, 7).
Δx = 9 − 1 = 8 and Δy = 7 − 1 = 6. 8² + 6² = 64 + 36 = 100 → d = √100 = 10
Find the distance between (−2, 3) and (3, −1). Round to the nearest tenth (or give the exact √ answer).
Δx = |3 − (−2)| = 5 and Δy = |−1 − 3| = 4. 5² + 4² = 25 + 16 = 41 → d = √41 ≈ 6.403 ≈ 6.4
Point P is in Quadrant I. It is 13 units from (0, 0), and its x-coordinate is 5. What is its y-coordinate?
The distance 13 is the hypotenuse and 5 is one leg. 5² + y² = 13² → 25 + y² = 169 → y² = 144 → y = 12. So P = (5, 12).

A right triangle has legs 5 and 12 and hypotenuse 13. Which equation is true?
The two legs go on the left. The hypotenuse goes alone on the right: 5² + 12² = 13² (25 + 144 = 169). 5² + 12² = 13²
The legs of a right triangle are 15 and 20. Find the hypotenuse.
15² + 20² = c² → 225 + 400 = 625 → c = √625 = 25
A triangle has sides 12, 16, and 20. Is it a right triangle?
12² + 16² = 144 + 256 = 400 and 20² = 400. Equal, so Yes.
One leg is 9 and the hypotenuse is 41. Find the other leg.
9² + b² = 41² → 81 + b² = 1681 → b² = 1600 → b = √1600 = 40
The legs are 2 and 5. Find the hypotenuse. Round to the nearest tenth (or give the exact √ answer).
2² + 5² = c² → 4 + 25 = 29 → c = √29 ≈ 5.385 ≈ 5.4
A triangle has sides 10, 24, and 25. Is it a right triangle?
10² + 24² = 100 + 576 = 676, but 25² = 625. 676 ≠ 625, so No.
A boat sails 7 miles east, then 24 miles north. How far is it from where it started?
The straight-line distance is the hypotenuse. 7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625 → √625 = 25 miles
Find the distance between (−3, 2) and (3, 10).
Δx = |3 − (−3)| = 6 and Δy = |10 − 2| = 8. 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100 → d = √100 = 10
A square has a diagonal of 10 cm. How long is each side? Round to the nearest tenth (or give the exact √ answer).
Both legs are the same length, s. s² + s² = 10² → 2s² = 100 → s² = 50 → s = √50 ≈ 7.071 ≈ 7.1 cm
A triangle has corners at (0, 0), (4, 0), and (4, 3). What is its perimeter?
Bottom side: 4. Right side: 3. Slanted side: 4² + 3² = 16 + 9 = 25 → √25 = 5. Perimeter = 4 + 3 + 5 = 12
Explain how you know when to ADD the squares and when to SUBTRACT them.
If you know both legs and want the hypotenuse, ADD: c² = a² + b². If you know the hypotenuse and one leg, SUBTRACT: a² = c² − b².
