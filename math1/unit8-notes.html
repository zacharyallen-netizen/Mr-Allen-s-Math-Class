1 Midpoint, Endpoint & Partition

📖 New words
segment
the straight piece of a line between two points. The two points are its endpoints.
midpoint
the point exactly halfway between the endpoints.
average
add the numbers and divide by how many there are. The midpoint averages the x’s and averages the y’s.
partition
split a segment into parts. A ratio like 1 : 3 means 1 part to 3 parts (4 parts in all).
📌 Midpoint = average of the coordinates: M = ( x₁ + x₂2 , y₁ + y₂2 ). To find a missing endpoint, work backward.
Midpoint of A(−2, 1) and B(4, 5) is M(1, 3): go halfway across (+3 of 6) and halfway up (+2 of 4).
🧭 How to find a midpoint
  1. Add the two x-values. Divide by 2.
  2. Add the two y-values. Divide by 2.
  3. Write the answer as a point (x, y).

Midpoint of (2, 3) and (8, 7)

Step 1. Average the x’s: 2 + 82 = click to reveal 102 = 5
Step 2. Average the y’s: 3 + 72 = click to reveal 102 = 5
✓ Average each: (2+82 , 3+72) = click to reveal (5, 5)
M(5, 5) is halfway between
🧭 How to find a missing endpoint
  1. Find the jump from the endpoint to the midpoint (change in x, change in y).
  2. Make the SAME jump again, starting at the midpoint.
  3. Where you land is the other endpoint.

Worked example: A(1, 1) is an endpoint. M(3, 4) is the midpoint. Find B.

Step 1. Jump from A to M: click to reveal x: 1 → 3 is +2. y: 1 → 4 is +3.
Step 2. Same jump from M: click to reveal x: 3 + 2 = 5. y: 4 + 3 = 7.
✓ Step 3. Other endpoint: click to reveal B(5, 7)
Jump +2, +3 twice: A → M → B
🧭 How to partition a segment in a ratio a : b
  1. Find the fraction of the way: aa + b.
  2. Find the change: Δx = x₂ − x₁ and Δy = y₂ − y₁.
  3. Multiply each change by the fraction.
  4. Add the results to the starting point A.

Worked example: A(1, 1), B(9, 5). Find P so that AP : PB = 1 : 3.

Step 1. Fraction of the way: click to reveal 11 + 3 = 14
Step 2. Changes: click to reveal Δx = 9 − 1 = 8, Δy = 5 − 1 = 4
Step 3. Multiply by ¼: click to reveal 8 × ¼ = 2, 4 × ¼ = 1
✓ Step 4. Add to A: click to reveal P = (1 + 2, 1 + 1) = (3, 2)
AB cut into 4 equal parts; P is 1 part from A

2 Distance Formula

📖 New words
Δ (“delta”)
means “change in.” Δx is the change in x (how far across). Δy is the change in y (how far up or down).
legs
the two sides of a right triangle that make the square corner.
hypotenuse
the longest side of a right triangle, across from the square corner. Here it is the distance d.
√ (square root)
the number that times itself gives the number inside. √25 = 5 because 5 × 5 = 25.
📌 d = √(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)² — the Pythagorean Theorem on the plane: legs Δx and Δy, hypotenuse d.
From (1,1) to (4,5): legs 3 and 4 → d = 5
🧭 How to find the distance between two points
  1. Find Δx: subtract the x-values. (Watch for minus a negative!)
  2. Find Δy: subtract the y-values.
  3. Square both. Add them.
  4. Take the square root. That is d.

Distance between (1, 2) and (4, 6)

Step 1. Δx = 3, Δy = 4: click to reveal √(3² + 4²) = √(9 + 16) = √25
✓ Step 2. d = click to reveal 5
Legs 3 and 4, hypotenuse 5

Worked example: distance between (−2, −1) and (4, 2)

Step 1. Δx: click to reveal 4 − (−2) = 4 + 2 = 6
Step 2. Δy: click to reveal 2 − (−1) = 3
Step 3. Square and add: click to reveal 6² + 3² = 36 + 9 = 45
✓ Step 4. Square root: click to reveal d = √45 ≈ 6.71

3 Perimeter & Area on the Plane

📖 New words
perimeter
the distance all the way around a figure. Add all the side lengths.
area
the space inside a figure, in square units.
base and height
for a triangle, the height must make a square corner with the base.
📌 Use the distance formula for side lengths, then apply the area formula (rectangle, triangle, etc.). Perimeter = add all the sides.
🧭 How to find perimeter and area from coordinates
  1. Plot the points and connect them in order.
  2. Find each side length. Count if the side is flat or straight up. Use the distance formula if it slants.
  3. Perimeter: add all the sides.
  4. Area: rectangle = length × width. Triangle = 12 × base × height.
  5. Write square units for area.

A rectangle has width 5 and height 3. Find its area.

For example, the rectangle A(1, 1), B(6, 1), C(6, 4), D(1, 4)

Step 2. Perimeter = 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = click to reveal 16 units
✓ Area = w · h = 5 · 3 = click to reveal 15 square units
15 unit squares fit inside

4 Parallel & Perpendicular Slopes

📖 New words
slope (m)
how steep a line is: rise ÷ run. m = y₂ − y₁x₂ − x₁.
parallel
lines that never meet. They have the SAME slope.
perpendicular
lines that meet at a right angle (a square corner).
opposite reciprocal
flip the fraction AND change the sign. The opposite reciprocal of ⅔ is −³⁄₂. Their product is −1.
📌 Parallel → SAME slope. Perpendicular → OPPOSITE RECIPROCAL slopes (their product is −1).
Two parallel lines (slope 2) and a perpendicular one (slope −½)
🧭 How to find slopes
  1. Slope from two points: m = y₂ − y₁x₂ − x₁.
  2. Parallel slope: keep the same number.
  3. Perpendicular slope: flip the fraction, then change the sign.
  4. Check perpendicular: multiply the two slopes. You should get −1.

Worked example: the line through (1, 2) and (4, 8)

Step 2. Parallel slope: click to reveal 2 (same)
Step 3. Perpendicular slope: click to reveal flip 21 → 12, change sign → −½
✓ Step 4. Check: click to reveal 2 × (−½) = −1 ✓
rise 6, run 3: slope 2

5 Equations of Parallel/Perp Lines

📖 New words
y = mx + b
slope-intercept form. m is the slope. b is the y-intercept (where the line crosses the y-axis).
📌 Keep the slope (parallel) or take the opposite reciprocal (perpendicular), then write the equation through the given point.
🧭 How to write the equation
  1. Find the slope of the given line (the number in front of x).
  2. Parallel: keep it. Perpendicular: flip it and change the sign.
  3. Put the point (x, y) and the new slope into y = mx + b.
  4. Solve for b.
  5. Write y = mx + b with your m and b.

Line through (0, 1) parallel to y = 2x − 5

Given line (slope 2) and the point (0, 1) the new line must go through
Parallel → same slope m = 2; through (0,1) → b = 1: click to reveal y = 2x + 1
Same slope, so the lines never meet

Line through (0, 1) perpendicular to y = 2x − 5

The given slope is 2 (the number in front of x). Flip it and change the sign.
Perpendicular → slope = −½; through (0,1): click to reveal y = −½x + 1
The lines meet at a square corner

6 Classifying Polygons

📖 New words
quadrilateral
a shape with 4 sides.
parallelogram
both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
rectangle
a parallelogram with 4 right angles.
rhombus
a parallelogram with 4 equal sides.
square
a rectangle AND a rhombus: 4 right angles and 4 equal sides.
trapezoid
exactly one pair of parallel sides.

🧭 How to classify a quadrilateral from its corners
  1. Find the slope of each side: AB, BC, CD, DA.
  2. Opposite sides with equal slopes are parallel.
  3. Neighbor sides whose slopes multiply to −1 are perpendicular (right angle).
  4. Use the distance formula to compare side lengths.
  5. Use the decision steps above to name it.

Worked example: A(1, 0), B(4, 1), C(3, 4), D(0, 3)

Step 3. Right angles? click to reveal ⅓ × (−3) = −1 → yes, a rectangle
Step 4. Side lengths: click to reveal AB = √(9 + 1) = √10, BC = √(1 + 9) = √10 … all √10
✓ Step 5. Name it: click to reveal right angles + equal sides → a square
