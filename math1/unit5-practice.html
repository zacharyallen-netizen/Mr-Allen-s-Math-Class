A Frequency & Distribution

Each bar counts the students whose score is in that interval. The height of the bar is the frequency (how many).

Part D · Residuals & Correlation vs. Causation

Type your answer and check it. You can also click Reveal to check your reasoning.

D Interpreting the Model

RESIDUAL A student studied 4 hours and scored 82. The trend line predicts 6(4) + 60 = 84. What is the residual (actual − predicted)?
Residual = actual − predicted = 82 − 84 = −2.
CAUSATION A study finds a strong positive correlation between ice cream sales and drowning incidents. Does this mean ice cream sales cause drownings? Explain.
No — correlation does not imply causation. A lurking variable, warm weather, likely increases both ice cream sales and swimming (and therefore drownings).
RESIDUAL A different student studied 2 hours and scored 75. The trend line predicts 6(2)+60=72. Find the residual, and state whether the trend line under- or over-predicted.
Residual = 75 − 72 = +3. Since the residual is positive, the trend line under-predicted this student's score.
Did the trend line under-predict or over-predict this student’s score?

Part E · Center & Spread on a Dot Plot

A player scored these points in 8 games: 4, 6, 6, 7, 8, 8, 8, 9.

E Mean, Median & MAD

Remember: mean = add all, then divide by how many. MAD = the average distance from the mean.
1 Find the mean.
4 + 6 + 6 + 7 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 9 = 56. 56 ÷ 8 → 7
2 Find the median.
8 values → average the 4th and 5th: (7 + 8) ÷ 2 → 7.5
3 Find the MAD (mean absolute deviation).
Distances from 7: 3, 1, 1, 0, 1, 1, 1, 2. Total 10. 10 ÷ 8 → 1.25
4 Player B scored 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 10. Both players have a mean of 7. Which player is more consistent (less spread out)?
Player A’s MAD is 1.25. Player B’s MAD is 18 ÷ 8 = 2.25. Smaller MAD = less spread → Player A
