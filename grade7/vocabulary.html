Unit 1 — Operations with Rational Numbers
Integera whole number or its opposite: … −2, −1, 0, 1, 2 …
Rational numbera number that can be written as a fraction of two integers
Oppositestwo numbers the same distance from 0 on different sides, like 5 and −5
Absolute valuethe distance from a number to 0; |−7| = 7
Additive inversethe opposite of a number; a number plus its additive inverse is 0
Terminating decimala decimal that ends, like 0.375
Repeating decimala decimal with digits that repeat forever, like 0.333…
Differencethe answer to a subtraction problem.
Distance between two numbersthe absolute value of their difference: |a − b|.
Productthe answer to a multiplication problem.
Quotientthe answer to a division problem.
Undefinedhas no answer. Dividing by 0 is undefined.
Bar notationa bar over the digits that repeat: 0.6̅ = 0.666…
Depositmoney put in (positive).
Withdrawalmoney taken out (negative).
Elevationheight above (+) or below (−) sea level.
Unit 2 — Expressions
Expressionnumbers, variables and operations with no equal sign, like 3x + 5
Terma part of an expression joined by + or −
Coefficientthe number multiplied by a variable; in −4x it is −4
Constanta term with no variable
Like termsterms with the same variable part, like 2x and −7x
Distributive propertya(b + c) = ab + ac
Factorwrite an expression as a product, like 6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3)
Equivalent expressionsexpressions that are equal for every value of the variable
Expandrewrite a product as a sum, like 3(x + 2) = 3x + 6.
Greatest common factor (GCF)the biggest number that divides every term.
Factor (verb)write as a product. Factoring undoes distributing.
Sum of expressionsadd them, then combine like terms.
Difference of expressionssubtract EVERY term of the second expression.
Unit 3 — Equations and Inequalities
Equationa math sentence that says two things are equal
Solutiona value of the variable that makes the sentence true
Inverse operationsoperations that undo each other, like + and −, or × and ÷
Two-step equationan equation that takes two moves to solve, like 3x + 7 = 22
Inequalitya math sentence with <, >, ≤ or ≥
Open circleon a graph, the number is NOT a solution (< or >)
Closed circleon a graph, the number IS a solution (≤ or ≥)
At least / at mostat least means ≥; at most means ≤
p(x + q) = ran equation with a number times a group.
Variablea letter that stands for the number you do not know yet.
Reasonablethe answer makes sense in the story (no −3 tickets!).
At least≥ (that much or more)
At most / no more than≤ (that much or less)
More than>
Less than / fewer than<
Unit 4 — Proportional Relationships
Ratioa comparison of two quantities, like 3 to 4
Ratea ratio of two different units, like 60 miles in 2 hours
Unit ratea rate for ONE unit, like 30 miles per 1 hour
Complex fractiona fraction with a fraction in the top, bottom or both
Proportionaltwo quantities with the same ratio y/x every time
Constant of proportionality (k)the unit rate k = y/x in y = kx
Originthe point (0, 0) on a graph
(1, r)the point where x = 1. Its y-value r is the unit rate.
y = kxthe equation of a proportional relationship. k is the constant of proportionality.
Proportiontwo equal ratios, like 3/4 = 9/12.
Unit 5 — Percents
Percentper hundred; 35% = 35/100 = 0.35
Part, percent, wholepart = percent × whole
Taxa percent of the price added to the cost
Tip (gratuity)a percent of the bill added for service
Discount / markdowna percent taken off the price
Markupa percent added to what the store paid
Commissiona percent of sales that a salesperson earns
Simple interestI = Prt (principal × rate × time in years)
Percent changechange ÷ original × 100
Percent error|estimate − actual| ÷ actual × 100
Wholethe total amount (100%).
Partthe piece that is a percent of the whole.
Tax / tip / feea percent ADDED to the cost.
Principal (P)the money you start with (save or borrow).
Rate (r)the yearly percent, as a decimal.
Time (t)in years.
Simple interest (I)I = P × r × t
Absolute valuedistance from 0, so the error is never negative.
Unit 6 — Scale Drawings, Angles and Triangles
Scalea ratio that compares drawing length to actual length, like 1 in : 4 ft
Scale factorthe number you multiply by to go from one size to the other
Triangle inequalitythe two shorter sides must add to MORE than the longest side
Complementary anglestwo angles that add to 90°
Supplementary anglestwo angles that add to 180°
Vertical anglesopposite angles made by two crossing lines; they are equal
Adjacent anglesangles that share a side and a vertex and do not overlap
Areathe space inside, in square units.
Angle sumthe three angles of a triangle add to 180°.
Complementaryadd to 90°.
Supplementaryadd to 180° (a straight line).
Unknown anglean angle written with a variable, like (2x + 6)°.
Unit 7 — Circles, Area, Surface Area and Volume
Radius (r)from the center to the edge of a circle
Diameter (d)across the circle through the center; d = 2r
Circumference (C)the distance around a circle; C = πd = 2πr
π (pi)the circumference ÷ the diameter of ANY circle, about 3.14
Area of a circleA = πr²
Composite shapea shape made of simpler shapes
Surface areathe total area of all the faces of a 3-D shape
Volumethe space inside a 3-D shape; V = Bh for a prism (B = area of the base)
Radiuscenter to edge.
Diameteredge to edge through the center. d = 2r.
Circumferencethe distance around.
πabout 3.14. For EVERY circle, C ÷ d = π.
Areathe space inside, in square units.
r²r × r, not r × 2.
TrapezoidA = ½(b₁ + b₂)h
Facea flat side of a 3-D shape.
Neta 3-D shape unfolded flat.
Base (B)the area of the shape on the end of the prism.
Unit 8 — Statistics
Populationthe whole group you want to know about
Samplea part of the population that you actually ask or measure
Random sampleevery member of the population has the same chance to be picked
Biased samplea sample that leans one way and does not represent the population
Meanthe sum ÷ how many
Medianthe middle value in order
Rangegreatest − least
Interquartile range (IQR)Q3 − Q1, the spread of the middle half
Mean absolute deviation (MAD)the average distance of the data from the mean
Biasedleans one way; not fair.
Inferencea conclusion about the population from a sample.
IQRQ3 − Q1.
MADaverage distance from the mean.
Overlaphow much the two groups share the same values.
Difference of means in MADs(difference of the means) ÷ MAD. Bigger = less overlap.
Comparative inferencea conclusion that compares two populations using random samples from each.
Unit 9 — Probability
Probabilitya number from 0 to 1 that tells how likely an event is
Outcomeone possible result
Eventone or more outcomes
Sample spacethe list of ALL possible outcomes
Experimental probabilitytimes it happened ÷ total trials
Theoretical probabilityfavorable outcomes ÷ all equally likely outcomes
Compound eventtwo or more events together, like a coin AND a number cube
Simulationan experiment that models a real situation
Impossibleprobability 0.
Certainprobability 1.
Equally likelyprobability 1/2.
Trialone try of an experiment.
Relative frequencyanother name for experimental probability.
Probability modela list of outcomes and their probabilities.
