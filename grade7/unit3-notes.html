Unit 3 Guided Notes — Equations and Inequalities

Solve two-step equations and inequalities, graph the answers, and use them to solve real problems.

By the end of this unit, I can…
  • I can solve two-step equations like 3x + 7 = 22 and x/4 − 2 = 5
  • I can solve equations with parentheses like 4(x − 2) = 20
  • I can solve equations with fractions and decimals, and write equations for word problems
  • I can solve an inequality, flip the sign when I multiply or divide by a negative, and graph it

1 Two-Step Equations NC.7.EE.4

📖 Words to know
Inverse operations: operations that undo each other: + and −, × and ÷.
Solution: the value of x that makes the equation true.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: Keep the equation balanced: whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Undo in reverse order: first undo the + or −, then undo the × or ÷. Check by putting your answer back in.
3x + 7 = 22. Take away the 7: three x's are 15. One x is 5.

To solve 3x + 7 = 22, first ? from both sides, then ?.

To solve x/4 − 2 = 5, first ?, then ?.

Example: 3x + 7 = 22

Example: x/4 − 2 = 5

1Undo − 2: add 2 to both sides. x/4 = 7
2Undo ÷ 4: multiply both sides by 4. x = 28
3Check: 28 ÷ 4 − 2 = 7 − 2 = 5 ✓ x = 28
⚠ Watch out: Undo the + or − FIRST. In 2x + 6 = 20, dividing only the 2x by 2 breaks the balance.
Solve: 5x + 8 = 43
5x + 8 − 8 = 43 − 8, so 5x = 35. Then x = 35 ÷ 5 = 7.
Solve: x/3 − 4 = 2
x/3 − 4 + 4 = 2 + 4, so x/3 = 6. Multiply by 3: x = 18.
Kim solved 2x + 6 = 20 like this: x + 6 = 10, so x = 4. What went wrong?
Dividing must happen to EVERY term. Subtract 6 first: 2x = 14, so x = 7.

2 Equations with Parentheses NC.7.EE.4

📖 Words to know
p(x + q) = r: an equation with a number times a group.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: Two ways work. Way 1: distribute, then solve the two-step equation. Way 2: divide both sides by the number outside first. Pick the easier way.

Way 1: 4(x − 2) = 20 → 4x − ? = 20 → 4x = ? → x = ?

Way 2: 4(x − 2) = 20 → x − 2 = ? → x = ?

Example (distribute first): 4(x − 2) = 20

Example (divide first): −3(x + 5) = 12

1Divide both sides by −3: x + 5 = −4
2Subtract 5: x = −9
3Check: −3(−9 + 5) = −3(−4) = 12 ✓ x = −9
⚠ Watch out: When you distribute, multiply EVERY term inside. −3(x + 5) is −3x − 15, not −3x + 5.
Solve: 2(x + 6) = 30
x + 6 = 15, so x = 9.
Solve: −5(x − 3) = 35
Divide by −5: x − 3 = −7. Add 3: x = −4.
A rectangle is 5 cm wide and (x + 3) cm long. Its perimeter is 34 cm, so 2(x + 3) + 10 = 34. What is x?
2(x + 3) = 24, so x + 3 = 12, and x = 9. The length is 12 cm.

3 Equations with Fractions, Decimals and Word Problems NC.7.EE.3

📖 Words to know
Variable: a letter that stands for the number you do not know yet.
Reasonable: the answer makes sense in the story (no −3 tickets!).
📌 THE BIG IDEA: Fractions and decimals do not change the steps. For word problems: 1 name the variable, 2 find the rate (× x) and the one-time amount (+ or −), 3 write the equation, 4 solve, 5 check that the answer makes sense.

A gym costs $25 to join plus $12 a month. Total $97: ?m + ? = 97

The rate goes with the variable. The one-time amount is the ?.

Example: 0.75x + 4.5 = 12

Example: a gym costs $25 to join plus $12 a month. Maya paid $97. How many months?

1Let m = number of months.
2Rate: $12 each month → 12m. One time: $25.
312m + 25 = 97
412m = 72, so m = 6
5Check: 12(6) + 25 = 97 ✓ 6 months
⚠ Watch out: Read for the RATE ("each", "per", "every"). The rate is multiplied by the variable. The one-time fee is not.
Solve: 0.5x + 3 = 9
0.5x = 6, so x = 6 ÷ 0.5 = 12.
A taxi charges $3.50 plus $2.25 per mile. A ride cost $19.25. Which equation fits? (m = miles)
$2.25 per mile → 2.25m. Plus $3.50 once. Total 19.25. (Solving: m = 7 miles.)
Jen has $40. She buys a $7.75 binder and 3 notebooks that cost the same. She has $14.25 left. How much is one notebook (in dollars)?
She spent 40 − 14.25 = 25.75. Take away the binder: 25.75 − 7.75 = 18. Each notebook: 18 ÷ 3 = $6.

4 Solving and Graphing Inequalities NC.7.EE.4

📖 Words to know
Inequality: a sentence with <, >, ≤ or ≥. It has MANY solutions.
Open circle: for < or >: the number itself is NOT a solution.
Closed circle: for ≤ or ≥: the number IS a solution.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: Solve an inequality like an equation, with one new rule: when you multiply or divide by a negative number, flip the inequality sign. Then graph: circle at the number, shade toward the solutions.
x > 3: open circle at 3 (3 is not a solution), shaded to the right.
InequalitySay itCircleShade
x > 3more than 3??
x ≤ −1at most −1??
x ≥ 4at least 4??
x < 0less than 0??

Example: 3x + 2 > 11

Example: −2x + 5 ≤ 13 (divide by a negative)

⚠ Watch out: Only multiplying or dividing by a NEGATIVE flips the sign. Adding or subtracting a negative does not.
Solve: 4x − 3 < 17. The answer is x < □. What number goes in the box?
4x < 20, so x < 5.
Solve: −3x + 4 ≥ 19
−3x ≥ 15. Divide by −3 and flip: x ≤ −5.
Which graph shows the solutions of 6 − 2x > 10?
−2x > 4. Divide by −2 and flip: x < −2. Open circle at −2, shade left.

5 Inequality Word Problems NC.7.EE.4

📖 Words to know
At least: ≥ (that much or more)
At most / no more than: ≤ (that much or less)
More than: >
Less than / fewer than: <
📌 THE BIG IDEA: Write the inequality from the story, solve it, then ask: does the answer make sense? If you can only buy whole things, round to a whole number the story allows.

"You can spend at most $60" uses the sign ?.

"She needs at least 80 points" uses the sign ?.

Example: you have $60. Tickets cost $11 each and snacks cost $9. How many tickets can you buy?

1Let t = tickets. Cost: 11t + 9. At most $60: 11t + 9 ≤ 60
211t ≤ 51, so t ≤ 4.6...
3You cannot buy part of a ticket. 5 tickets cost $64, too much.
4At most 4 tickets (0, 1, 2, 3 or 4)
⚠ Watch out: Round to fit the story. "At most 4.6 tickets" means 4. "At least 9.2 weeks" means 10 weeks.
A phone plan costs $20 plus $0.10 per text. You can spend at most $35. What is the greatest number of texts you can send?
0.10t ≤ 15, so t ≤ 150. At most 150 texts.
Leo has 32 points and earns 6 points per quiz. He needs MORE than 80 points. Which inequality fits? (q = quizzes)
6q + 32 > 80.
Ana has $40 and saves $12 each week. She needs at least $150 for a bike. What is the fewest whole weeks she must save?
12w ≥ 110, so w ≥ 9.17. 9 weeks gives $148, not enough. She needs 10 weeks.
