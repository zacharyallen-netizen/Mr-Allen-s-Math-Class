1 The Probability Scale NC.7.SP.5

📖 Words to know
Impossible: probability 0.
Certain: probability 1.
Equally likely: probability 1/2.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: Probability is a number from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). Near 0 is unlikely. 1/2 is as likely as not. Near 1 is likely. It can be a fraction, a decimal or a percent.

2 Experimental Probability NC.7.SP.6

Trial: one try of an experiment.
Experimental probability: times it happened ÷ number of trials.
Relative frequency: another name for experimental probability.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: Experimental P = times it happened ÷ total trials. Use it to predict: multiply the probability by the number of future trials. The more trials, the closer it usually gets to the theoretical probability.

28 ÷ 50 = 0.56.
Tia hit the target 4 times in 10 throws. About how many hits in 200 throws?
0.4 × 200 = 80 hits.
A number cube was rolled 300 times. A 5 came up 72 times. Theory says P(5) = 1/6. How many MORE 5s came up than theory predicts?
Theory: 50. Actual: 72. 72 − 50 = 22 more.

3 Theoretical Probability NC.7.SP.7

📖 Words to know
Theoretical probability: favorable outcomes ÷ total outcomes, when all outcomes are equally likely.
Probability model: a list of outcomes and their probabilities.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: P(event) = favorable outcomes ÷ total outcomes (when every outcome is equally likely). In a model, all the probabilities add to 1. P(not A) = 1 − P(A).
An 8-part spinner with equal parts.

P(even) = 4/8 = ?

P(not 8) = 1 − 1/8 = ?

Example: a letter is picked at random from MATH. What is P(vowel)?

1Outcomes: M, A, T, H (4)
2Vowels: A (1)
3P(vowel) = 1/4
⚠ Watch out: Only use favorable ÷ total when the outcomes are EQUALLY likely. A spinner with one huge part is not.
A number cube (1–6) is rolled. What is P(number greater than 4)? (Type a fraction.)
5 and 6: 2 out of 6 = 1/3.
P(rain) = 0.35. What is P(no rain)?
1 − 0.35 = 0.65.
A bag has red, blue and green tiles. P(red) = 1/4 and P(blue) = 1/3. There are 24 tiles. How many are green?
P(green) = 12/12 − 3/12 − 4/12 = 5/12. (5/12) × 24 = 10 tiles.

5 Simulations and Comparing Results NC.7.SP.8

📖 Words to know
Simulation: an experiment that acts out a real situation with coins, number cubes, spinners or random numbers.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: Pick a tool whose chances MATCH the real situation. Run many trials. Use the results as an estimate. If experimental and theoretical results are far apart, run more trials or check the model.
Real situationChanceTool
A team wins half its games1/2?
1 in 6 cereal boxes has a prize1/6?
A player makes 3 of 4 shots3/4?

Example: 1 in 6 boxes has a prize. Simulate how many boxes you buy to get a prize.

1Roll a number cube. A 6 = prize.
2Count the rolls until you get a 6. That is one trial.
3Do many trials and find the average.
4Most trials take about 6 boxes, but some take 1 and some take 15!
⚠ Watch out: A simulation tool must have the SAME probability as the real thing. A coin cannot model a 1/6 chance.
A spinner was spun 120 times. Theory says P(blue) = 1/4. How many blues does theory predict?
120 ÷ 4 = 30.
Which tool can simulate a 1/6 chance that a box has a prize?
A number cube has 6 equally likely sides. One side = 1/6.
Ben flips a coin 10 times and gets 7 heads. Which is the best explanation?
Small samples vary a lot. With many more flips, the results usually get close to 1/2.
