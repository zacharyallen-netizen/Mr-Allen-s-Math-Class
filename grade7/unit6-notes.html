Unit 6 Guided Notes — Scale Drawings, Angles and Triangles

Use scale drawings, draw triangles from given parts, and use angle facts to find missing angles.

1 Scale Drawings: Lengths NC.7.G.1

📖 Words to know
Scale: drawing length : actual length, like 1 cm : 4 m.
Scale factor: how many times bigger (or smaller) one is than the other.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: Use the scale like a unit rate. If 1 cm stands for 4 m, then 6 cm stands for 6 × 4 = 24 m. To go from actual back to the drawing, divide.

Actual (m)4??30

Example: a map scale is 1 inch : 20 miles. Two towns are 3.5 inches apart. How far apart are they?

1Each inch is 20 miles.
23.5 × 20 = 70
370 miles
⚠ Watch out: Keep the units with the right side of the scale. Check: is the actual length bigger than the drawing (for a map)?
A scale is 1 cm : 4 m. A wall is 6 cm on the drawing. How long is the real wall, in meters?
6 × 4 = 24 m.
A map scale is 1 inch : 5 miles. Two parks are 30 miles apart. How far apart are they on the map?
30 ÷ 5 = 6 inches.
A model car is built with a scale of 1 : 24. The model is 7.5 inches long. How many FEET long is the real car?
7.5 × 24 = 180 inches. 180 ÷ 12 = 15 feet.

2 Scale Drawings: Area and New Scales NC.7.G.1

📖 Words to know
Area: the space inside, in square units.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: To find a real area, change each LENGTH with the scale first, then multiply. (Scaling the area by the scale factor alone is a common mistake: area grows by the scale factor times itself.)

A 2 cm by 3 cm room, scale 1 cm : 2 m. Real size: ? m by ? m. Real area: ? m²

Redraw a 12 cm line at half the size: ? cm

Example: a garden is drawn 4 in by 6 in. The scale is 1 in : 3 ft. What is the real area?

1Real length: 6 × 3 = 18 ft. Real width: 4 × 3 = 12 ft.
2Area = 18 × 12
3216 ft² (not 4 × 6 × 3 = 72)
⚠ Watch out: For area, scale BOTH lengths. Multiplying the drawing area by the scale only once gives a number that is too small.
A room is drawn 5 in by 4 in. The scale is 1 in : 2 ft. What is the real WIDTH (from 4 in), in feet?
4 × 2 = 8 ft.
A square is drawn 2 cm on each side. The scale is 1 cm : 3 m. What is the real area?
Each side is 6 m. 6 × 6 = 36 m².
A poster is 16 in wide. Lin redraws it with a scale factor of 1/4. How many SQUARE inches is a 16 in by 16 in square on the new drawing?
Each side is 4 in. 4 × 4 = 16 square inches.

3 Drawing Triangles NC.7.G.2

📖 Words to know
Triangle inequality: the two SHORTER sides must add up to MORE than the longest side.
Angle sum: the three angles of a triangle add to 180°.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: Three sides: if the two shorter sides add to more than the longest, they make exactly ONE triangle; if not, NO triangle. Three angles: if they add to 180°, they make MANY triangles (same shape, any size); if not, NO triangle.
3 + 4 = 7, which is more than 5. These sides make exactly one triangle.

Example: can 4 in, 4 in and 9 in make a triangle?

1The two shorter sides: 4 + 4 = 8
28 is less than 9. The short sides cannot reach.
3No triangle.
⚠ Watch out: Add the two SHORTEST sides. And "equal to" is not enough: 2 + 3 = 5 lies flat and makes no triangle.
Two angles of a triangle are 55° and 75°. What is the third angle, in degrees?
180 − 55 − 75 = 50°.
How many triangles can you make with sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 12 cm?
5 + 6 = 11, which is less than 12. No triangle.
Which set of angles makes MANY different triangles?
45 + 45 + 90 = 180. Three angles that work make many triangles (same shape, different sizes).

4 Angle Relationships NC.7.G.5

📖 Words to know
Complementary: add to 90°.
Supplementary: add to 180° (a straight line).
Vertical angles: across from each other where two lines cross. They are EQUAL.
Adjacent angles: next to each other: same vertex, one shared side.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: Look for the picture: a right angle (square corner) → 90°. A straight line → 180°. Two crossing lines → opposite angles are equal.
Vertical angles are equal (40° and 40°). Angles next to each other on a line are supplementary: 40° + 140° = 180°.

The complement of 35° is ?°.

The supplement of 112° is ?°.

The angle vertical to a 74° angle is ?°.

Example: two angles make a right angle. One is 48°. Find the other.

1Right angle → they add to 90°.
290 − 48 = 42
342°
⚠ Watch out: Complementary = 90 (C comes before S, and 90 comes before 180). Supplementary = 180.
What is the supplement of a 62° angle, in degrees?
180 − 62 = 118°.
Two lines cross. One angle is 65°. What is the angle across from it (vertical to it)?
Vertical angles are equal: 65°.
Angle A and angle B are complementary. Angle B and angle C are supplementary. Angle A = 30°. What is angle C?
B = 90 − 30 = 60°. C = 180 − 60 = 120°.

5 Equations for Unknown Angles NC.7.G.5

📖 Words to know
Unknown angle: an angle written with a variable, like (2x + 6)°.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: 1 Name the relationship (90°, 180° or equal). 2 Write the equation. 3 Solve for x. 4 Put x back in to find the angle the question asks for.
Two angles on a straight line are supplementary.

Example: two complementary angles are (2x + 6)° and 2x°. Find x.

1Complementary → they add to 90.
22x + 6 + 2x = 90
34x + 6 = 90, so 4x = 84
4x = 21. The angles are 48° and 42°. Check: 48 + 42 = 90 ✓
⚠ Watch out: After you find x, read the question again. It may ask for the ANGLE, not x.
Two supplementary angles are 4x° and 20°. What is x?
4x = 160, so x = 40.
Two angles on a straight line are (3x + 30)° and 2x°. Which equation fits?
They add to 180: 3x + 30 + 2x = 180. (5x = 150, x = 30.)
Vertical angles measure (4x + 10)° and (3x + 25)°. What is the measure of ONE of the angles, in degrees?
4x + 10 = 3x + 25, so x = 15. The angle is 4(15) + 10 = 70°. Check: 3(15) + 25 = 70°.
