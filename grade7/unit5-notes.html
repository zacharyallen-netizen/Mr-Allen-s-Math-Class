1 Part, Percent, Whole NC.7.RP.3

📖 Words to know
Percent: out of 100. 30% = 30/100 = 0.30.
Whole: the total amount (100%).
Part: the piece that is a percent of the whole.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: part = percent × whole. Change the percent to a decimal. If you know the part and the percent, divide: whole = part ÷ percent. To find the percent, divide part ÷ whole.
30% of 80: 0.30 × 80 = 24.

2w = 18 ÷ 0.40

⚠ Watch out: Change the percent to a decimal before you multiply: 30% is 0.30, not 30.
What is 35% of 60?
0.35 × 60 = 21.
9 is 15% of what number?
9 ÷ 0.15 = 60. Check: 0.15 × 60 = 9.
A class has 120 students. 75% of them came to the game. How many did NOT come?
100% − 75% = 25%. 0.25 × 120 = 30 students.

2 Percent Increase and Decrease NC.7.RP.3

📖 Words to know
Percent change: how much it changed compared with the ORIGINAL amount.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: percent change = change ÷ original × 100. If the amount went up, it is an increase. If it went down, it is a decrease. Always divide by the ORIGINAL (the starting) amount.

From 40 to 56: change = ?, 16 ÷ 40 = ? = ?% increase

From 50 to 35: change = ?, 15 ÷ 50 = ?% decrease

Example: a $68 game is marked down 25%. What is the new price?

1Way 1: 25% of 68 = 0.25 × 68 = 17. 68 − 17 = 51
2Way 2: you pay 75%: 0.75 × 68 = 51
3$51
⚠ Watch out: Divide by the ORIGINAL amount, not the new amount. From 40 to 56 is a 40% increase, not 16 ÷ 56.
A price goes from $24 to $30. What is the percent increase?
6 ÷ 24 = 0.25 = 25%.
A $40 shirt is 30% off. What is the sale price?
0.70 × 40 = $28.
A town grew from 1,200 people to 1,500 people. Then it shrank back to 1,200. Which is true?
Up: 300 ÷ 1,200 = 25%. Down: 300 ÷ 1,500 = 20%.

3 Tax, Tips, Fees, Markups and Commission NC.7.RP.3

📖 Words to know
Tax / tip / fee: a percent ADDED to the cost.
Markup: a percent ADDED to what the store paid.
Commission: a percent of sales that a worker EARNS.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: Find the percent of the amount, then add it (tax, tip, markup) or subtract it (discount). Shortcut: total with 6% tax = 1.06 × price. With a 20% discount: 0.80 × price.

$45 with 6% tax: 1.06 × 45 = ?

An 18% tip on a $24 bill: 0.18 × 24 = ?

A store pays $80 and marks it up 15%: 1.15 × 80 = ?

Example: an $80 jacket is 20% off. Then 5% tax is added. What is the total?

1Discount first: 0.80 × 80 = 64
2Then tax: 1.05 × 64 = 67.20
3$67.20
⚠ Watch out: Take the discount off first, then find the tax on the SALE price. Do not just add and subtract the percents.
Ana sells $1,200 in shoes. She earns 5% commission. How much commission does she earn (in dollars)?
0.05 × 1,200 = $60.
A meal costs $36. You leave a 15% tip. What is the total?
0.15 × 36 = 5.40. 36 + 5.40 = $41.40.
A store buys a bike for $85 and marks it up 20%. Later the bike is 20% off. What is the sale price in dollars?
1.20 × 85 = 102. 0.80 × 102 = $81.60. Not $85: the 20% off is taken from a bigger number.

4 Simple Interest NC.7.RP.3

📖 Words to know
Principal (P): the money you start with (save or borrow).
Rate (r): the yearly percent, as a decimal.
Time (t): in years.
Simple interest (I): I = P × r × t
📌 THE BIG IDEA: I = Prt. Interest is the extra money earned (or owed). The final balance is P + I. Time must be in years: 6 months = 0.5 year.

$500 at 4% for 3 years: I = 500 × ? × 3 = ?

The balance is 500 + 60 = ?

Example: you save $2,000 at 3% simple interest for 5 years. What is the balance?

1I = 2000 × 0.03 × 5 = 300
2Balance = 2000 + 300
3$2,300
⚠ Watch out: Use the rate as a decimal (3% = 0.03), and time in years (18 months = 1.5 years).
Find the simple interest on $250 at 4% for 2 years (in dollars).
250 × 0.04 × 2 = $20.
You borrow $3,000 at 6% simple interest for 6 months. How much interest do you owe?
3,000 × 0.06 × 0.5 = $90.
Lee earned $90 in simple interest on $1,500 in 2 years. What was the yearly interest rate, in percent?
1500 × 2 = 3000. r = 90 ÷ 3000 = 0.03 = 3%.

5 Percent Error NC.7.RP.3

📖 Words to know
Percent error: how far off an estimate or measurement is, compared with the actual value.
Absolute value: distance from 0, so the error is never negative.
📌 THE BIG IDEA: percent error = |estimate − actual| ÷ actual × 100. Always divide by the ACTUAL (true) value.

Guess 45, actual 50: |45 − 50| = ?, 5 ÷ 50 = ?%

Guess 132, actual 120: |132 − 120| = 12, 12 ÷ 120 = ?%

Example: Max guesses a jar has 9 marbles. It has 12. What is his percent error?

1Error: |9 − 12| = 3
23 ÷ 12 = 0.25
325%
⚠ Watch out: Divide by the ACTUAL value, not the guess.
Sam guesses a board is 19 inches. It is 20 inches. What is the percent error?
1 ÷ 20 = 0.05 = 5%.
Jo estimates 40 people will come. 32 come. What is the percent error?
8 ÷ 32 = 0.25 = 25%.
Two students measure a 40 cm desk. Ava gets 38 cm. Ben gets 41 cm. How much bigger (in percentage points) is Ava's percent error than Ben's?
Ava: 2 ÷ 40 = 5%. Ben: 1 ÷ 40 = 2.5%. 5 − 2.5 = 2.5.
